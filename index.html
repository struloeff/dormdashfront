How DormDash worked

A student-run delivery and rideshare service for Pepperdine, built and operated by one founder. Students ordered from any restaurant, a student driver bought and brought it, and the customer paid what the receipt actually said.

417
commits to the backend
10.5k
lines of Python
19
customer, driver & admin screens
22
dorms on the delivery map
  1. 1
    Order anything

    Pick any restaurant, list the items, choose a dorm. Search ran on Google Places with OpenStreetMap as the free fallback.

  2. 2
    Dispatch on Discord

    A Discord bot posted each order to verified student drivers; the first to claim it got the job.

  3. 3
    Confirm the real bill

    The driver entered prices from the actual receipt. California sales tax came from the CDTFA rate API for the restaurant's address.

  4. 4
    Pay through Stripe

    Customers paid by Stripe Checkout; drivers were paid out through Stripe Connect accounts they set up in their dashboard.

  5. 5
    Track & deliver

    Live status over WebSockets, a delivery photo at drop-off, then confirm, rate, or open a dispute.

🧱

Stack

Flask + Socket.IO on gunicorn/gevent, PostgreSQL, Stripe & Stripe Connect, Google Maps Platform, OSRM, discord.py, deployed on Render. Installable as a PWA.

🛡️

Abuse & cost controls

Cloudflare Turnstile, per-user and per-IP rate limits, automatic IP blocking, a budget guard on paid map APIs, and an admin killswitch.

🧭

Operations

Driver sign-up with an e-signed contract, a driver dashboard for jobs and payouts, and an admin panel for disputes, refunds and platform revenue.

Driving for DormDash

Drivers were Pepperdine students earning between classes, with flexible hours and weekly Stripe payouts.

  • $18–$25 / hour
    Most of each fare and 100% of tips went to the driver
  • Work between classes
    Claim jobs from Discord when you're free, ignore them when you're not
  • A real contract
    Independent-contractor agreement, signed in the browser before the first job
